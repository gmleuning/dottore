# ---- Hegel ----
Def: The apodictic judgement states that the subject, as so and so constituted, is good or right. Its subject contains what it ought to be and also its constitution, which holds the ground why the predicate belongs to it or not. This judgement is truly objective, the truth of judgement in general: subject and predicate correspond and have the same concept. The fact is divided into ought and being and joins them, and this filled copula passes into the syllogism.
