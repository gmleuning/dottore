# ---- Hegel ----
Def: The mammals are the perfect animals: they have breasts, four articulated limbs and every organ developed. Because they suckle their young from themselves, they come to feel the unity of one individual with another, the genus, which exists in the offspring, though in nature this unity falls back into single individuals. They are classed truly not by marks we pick out but by the animal's own weapons, its teeth, feet and claws, by which it sets itself against its non-organic nature and shows itself a subject for itself. So there are four classes: animals with hands (man and the monkey), animals with claws (beasts of prey, rodents, bats, sloths), animals with fins (the Cetacea) and animals with hoofs (swine, elephants, cattle, horses), which can be tamed to work for man. Further subdivision is left to the contingency of nature, above all to climate.
Q:
  Mammals have breasts, four articulated extremities, and all their organs are developed. Because they have breasts, they suckle and feed their young from themselves. These animals thus attain to the feeling of the unity of the one individual with the other, to the feeling of the genus, which achieves an existence in the progeny in which the two individuals are precisely the genus, even though, in Nature, this unity of the individual with the genus falls back again into singularity.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
  Of all animals, monkeys are the most docile (bildsamsten) and have most affection for their young; the satisfied sexual instinct persists for them in objective form, for they have passed over into an other, and in caring for the needs of their young, they have the higher, disinterested intuition of this unity.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
  In mammals, the skin continues its growth into wool, hair, bristles, spines (in the hedgehog), and even into scales and armour (in the armadillo). Man, on the other hand, has a skin which is smooth, pure, and much more animalized and which also sheds anything of an osseous nature.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
  Further essential classifications have been based on the behaviour of animals as individuals to other animals: that is, on their teeth, feet, claws, and beaks. It was a correct instinct which led to the choice of these parts, for it is by them that animals themselves distinguish themselves from other animals: if the difference is to be a true one, it must not be simply a sign which we have picked out, but a difference of the animal itself. By opposing itself as an individual to its non-organic nature through its weapons, the animal demonstrates that it is a subject for itself.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
  On this basis, the classes of mammals are very accurately distinguished: αα. into animals whose feet are hands, man and the monkey (the monkey is a satire on man, a satire which it must amuse him to see if he does not take himself too seriously but is willing to laugh at himself); ββ. into animals whose extremities are claws, dogs, wild beasts like the lion, the king of beasts; γγ. into rodents, in which the teeth are specially shaped; δδ. into cheiroptera, which have a membrane stretched between the toes, as occurs even in some rodents (these animals come nearer to dogs and monkeys); εε. into sloths, in which some of the toes are missing altogether and have become claws; ζζ. into animals with fin-like limbs, the Cetacea; ηη. into hoofed animals, like swine, elephants (which have a trunk), horned cattle, horses, etc. The strength of these animals resides in the upper part of the body, most of them can be tamed to work; and the formation of the extremities shows a special relationship to the animals' non-organic nature.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
  If the animals under ββ, γγ, δδ, εε are grouped together as animals with claws, then we have four classes: 1. Animals with hands, 2. with claws, 3. with hooves, which work for man, 4. with fins.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
  [Lamarck, quoted:] The unguiculate mammals have four limbs, flat or pointed claws at the tips of their toes which latter do not envelop the claws. The limbs are, in general, fitted for seizing, or at least hanging on to, objects. These animals include those which are most highly organized. The hoofed mammals (Ungulata) have four limbs, the toes of which are completely surrounded at their extremities by a rounded, horny substance called a hoof (sabot). Their feet can be used only for walking or running on the ground; they cannot be used for climbing trees, or for seizing an object or prey, or for attacking and rending other animals. They are completely herbivorous. The mammals without hooves have only two extremities, and these are very short and flat, and shaped like fins. Their toes, covered by skin, have neither claws nor hooves (corne); they are the least highly organized of all the mammals. They have neither a pelvis, nor hind legs; they swallow their food without masticating it; lastly, they usually live in water but come up to the surface to breathe air.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
  As regards the further subdivisions, these must be left to the chance and contingency proper to Nature, that is, to be determined by external factors. Climate, however, still constitutes the great determining factor. In southern latitudes, the animal kingdom is particularized more according to differences of climate and country than it is in northern latitudes: thus the Asiatic and African elephants are essentially distinct from each other, while in America there are none; lions and tigers, etc., are similarly distinguished.
    — Encyclopedia §370, Addition (tr. Petry)
