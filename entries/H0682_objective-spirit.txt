# ---- Hegel ----
Def: Objective spirit is the free will that gives itself existence in an external world of its own making. Its freedom is realized first in the immediate existence of the person and its property (abstract right), then in the will reflected into itself (morality), and finally in the customs and institutions of family, civil society and state (ethical life), which are freedom become a world.
Texts:
  The Philosophy of Right
    §§34–360
Citations:
  Accordingly this stage is its abstractly self-related actuality, which is negative in contrast with reality—the inherently individual will of a subject. According to the moment of the particularity of the will it has in addition a content consisting of determinate aims, and, as exclusive individuality, it has this content at the same time as an external world directly confronting it.
    — The Philosophy of Right §34 (tr. Knox, rev. Houlgate)
  Ethical life is the Idea of freedom in that, on the one hand, it is the living good—the good endowed in self-consciousness with knowing and willing and actualized by self-conscious action—while, on the other hand, self-consciousness has in the ethical realm its foundation in and for itself and its motivating end.
    — The Philosophy of Right §142 (tr. Knox, rev. Houlgate)
