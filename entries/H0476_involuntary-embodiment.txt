# 476 Involuntary Embodiment

# ---- Hegel ----
Def: Involuntary embodiment is the bodily expression that inner sensations take on without the will, described already among the soul's sensations and shared in part with animals: tears, voice, laughter, sighs and the rest. It differs from the free embodiments of the actual soul, which give the human body a peculiar mental stamp. The line is not fixed, for some involuntary expressions can also be produced consciously and freely, as when the voice becomes speech or laughing turns into laughing at someone.
