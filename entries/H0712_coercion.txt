# ---- Hegel ----
Def: Coercion is force applied to the existence of a free will. Because the will is in itself free and cannot be coerced, coercion destroys itself in its concept, and this appears in reality in its being cancelled by a second coercion. The second coercion, which annuls the first, is not only lawful but necessary.
Texts:
  The Philosophy of Right
    §§90–94
Citations:
  That coercion is in its concept self-destructive is exhibited in reality by the fact that coercion is annulled by coercion; coercion is thus shown to be not only right under certain conditions but necessary, i.e. as a second act of coercion which is the annulment of one that has preceded.
    — The Philosophy of Right §93 (tr. Knox, rev. Houlgate)
