# ---- Hegel ----
Def: Alienation is the giving up of one's property, either by abandoning it or by yielding it to another's will. It is possible because the thing is mine only insofar as I have put my will into it; it is limited to things external by nature, since what constitutes my own personality and freedom cannot be alienated.
Texts:
  The Philosophy of Right
    §§65–70
Citations:
  The reason I can alienate my property is that it is mine only insofar as I put my will into it. Hence I may abandon (derelinquiere) as ownerless anything that I have or yield it to the will of another and so into his possession, provided always that the thing in question is a thing external by nature.
    — The Philosophy of Right §65 (tr. Knox, rev. Houlgate)
